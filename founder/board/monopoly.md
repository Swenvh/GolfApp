# Monopoly lens
Score: 4/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **The market is small and has clear edges, which this lens likes.** There are 263 NGF member clubs and 275 courses. The software is in Dutch and built around Dutch practice: introducé limits, iDEAL, direct debit, bar tab on account, and CSV import from E-Golf4U, Nexxchange and IntoGolf. A narrow niche like this can be dominated before anyone large bothers to look at it. The ceiling is low, though. If every NGF club paid the standard € 449/month, that would be about € 1.4M a year. This is a business to own, not one that grows big.
- **There is a possible wedge.** No commission ("the money is the club's"), a member app in the club's own brand, and "mission control" written for volunteers. Together these are a point of view that the incumbents may not share. Nobody has checked that yet.
- **Switching costs could protect it later.** Once member records, invoices, the bar tab and direct debit run through Greenside, leaving it is painful. The same lock-in is now what keeps clubs with the incumbents.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **Nothing here is 10x better, and the incumbents are already in place.** Clubs already run E-Golf4U, Nexxchange or IntoGolf. IntoGolf already publishes six to eight club-branded apps. GOLF.NL books tee times for free and has 325,300 active users. The brief describes a better-made version of things clubs already have. It does not describe a breakthrough. The proprietary technology is weak: the product was built with an AI coding assistant, so a competitor could copy the no-commission terms or the branded app within months.
2. **There is no plan for distribution.** The buyer is a club board that changes slowly and depends on volunteers. Every sale also needs a migration and a three-month pilot. Nobody knows what it costs to win a club or how long a sale takes. Two founders with zero paying clubs have not shown how the first 10 clubs will come, let alone the first 100.
3. **Nothing compounds.** Each club is a separate silo, so there is no network effect. No brand exists among club boards yet. Scale saves on code but not on sales or onboarding. In ten years the business has only switching costs to defend it, and the incumbents hold those today.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- What is your secret? What do you know about Dutch clubs that E-Golf4U, Nexxchange and IntoGolf do not?
- Which small group of clubs will you win nearly all of first: 9-hole clubs, a region, or clubs on one incumbent? Why that group?
- What do clubs pay the incumbents today, how long are their contracts, and how hard is it to switch? (/founder-competitors)
- Will members open a club's own app when GOLF.NL is on their phone and free? (/founder-consumer)
- What does it cost to win one club, and what does it cost to run one? (/founder-cfo)
- If IntoGolf drops its commission next year, what do you still have that it does not?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. Golfclub Zwolle goes live and converts to a paid yearly contract. The club's members must use the app next to GOLF.NL, measured as active members.
2. A competitor map with incumbent prices, contract terms and switching costs (/founder-competitors). It must show a gap the incumbents cannot close quickly.
3. A named beachhead of no more than about 20 clubs, with a written plan to sign at least 5 founding clubs. The plan must include the cost to win each club, checked against the price by /founder-cfo.
4. A written answer to the durability question: what this builds that a copycat cannot have in month six. Examples are data across clubs, integrations or a brand with club boards.
